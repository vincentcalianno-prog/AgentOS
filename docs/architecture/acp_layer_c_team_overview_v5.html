Gate 1
SCM Review Gate
Supply chain reviews and approves the LRS
The SCM team reviews the full Legal Reference Sheet. Confirms every recommendation makes sense for this supplier, project, and equipment scope. Flags any commercial concerns to legal.
  • Review each disposition and confidence tier
  • Confirm project and supplier overlays are correctly applied
  • Flag agent-reasoned items that need legal priority attention
  • Nothing proceeds to legal until SCM approves
📝 Feedback captured for agent learning
Every SCM disposition override and correction is logged as structured feedback — which clause, what SCM changed it to, and why. Agents learn from these corrections over time. Commercial context corrections (e.g. “this is a Pratt project — apply stricter delivery terms”) refine how overlays are applied in future rounds.
Gate 2
Legal Review Gate
Legal reviews and approves counter-language
After SCM approval, legal reviews and approves every counter-proposal. Edits agent-reasoned drafts. Escalates items needing further discussion.
  • Approves, edits, or rejects each counter line
  • Agent-reasoned items get priority attention — these have no playbook backing
  • Legal edits feed back into the playbook as Tier 2 evidence for future negotiations
  • Sandelin approves all position changes — Claude never does
📝 Feedback captured as Tier-2 playbook evidence
Every legal edit to counter-proposal language is captured as a RecommendationReview record — logging what the agent drafted, what legal changed it to, and the rationale. These become Tier-2 evidence that directly refines future recommendations for the same clause type. Accumulated edits surface as proposed playbook updates for Sandelin's approval.

How to read this
Level 1 — Executed agreement. The outcome. Every layer below contributes to producing this.
Level 2 — Negotiation decisions. The three clause-level outcomes Claude assigns and the team approves: Accept, Counter-Negotiate, Reject.
Level 3 — Context shaping decisions. The overlays and confidence signals that adjust how a position is applied. Same clause, different supplier or project = potentially different disposition.
Level 4 — Playbook positions. The structured knowledge Claude looks up: established positions, cross-clause rules, pre-approved counter-language, and the template version in play.
Level 5 — Agreement types. The foundation. Which contract type is being negotiated determines which playbook entries, overlays, and clauses are even in scope.

Reading the confidence tier on every recommendation

Every clause recommendation in the Legal Reference Sheet is tagged with one of three confidence tiers — so the SCM and legal teams immediately know how much scrutiny to apply.

✓
Playbook — Verified
Source: a closed, signed agreement. Antora has tested this position and it held. High confidence. The LRS cites the specific agreement (e.g. “MCM PO T&Cs, May 6”). Counter-language is drawn directly from pre-approved text.
⚠
Playbook — Provisional
Source: legal team guidance. Calibrated by Sandelin or outside counsel but not yet in a signed agreement. Real, considered positions — apply judgment before sending, especially on novel suppliers.
✗
Agent-reasoned
Source: Claude reasoning from general principles. No formal Antora position yet. Claude drafts a starting point but flags it clearly. Legal review required before use. These become candidates for new playbook entries.
